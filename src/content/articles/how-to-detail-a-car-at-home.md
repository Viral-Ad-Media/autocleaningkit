Professional detailers don't have secret products. What they have is a sequence: they clean in an order where each step sets up the next one and nothing they do later undoes something they did earlier. Follow the same nine steps at home and you'll get most of the way to a shop finish with a few hours and a modest kit.

Work in the shade, on a cool panel, and give yourself a full morning the first time.

## What you'll need

- Two buckets (one for soapy water, one for rinsing your mitt), ideally with grit guards
- A pH-neutral car shampoo and a microfiber or chenille wash mitt
- A dedicated wheel cleaner, a soft wheel brush and a stiff tire brush
- A clay bar or clay mitt with lubricant
- Plenty of clean microfiber towels, including one large drying towel
- Polish and an applicator pad (or a dual-action polisher)
- A wax, sealant or ceramic spray for protection
- A vacuum, an interior cleaner and a few soft [detailing brushes](/car-detailing-brushes-2026)

## Step 1: Clean the wheels and tires first

Wheels carry the heaviest grime on the car: brake dust, road film and tar. Clean them before the paint so you don't splash that dirt onto panels you've already washed. Spray a [wheel cleaner](/best-wheel-cleaners-for-cars-2026) on one wheel at a time, agitate the face and barrel with a soft brush, scrub the tire sidewall with a stiffer brush, then rinse thoroughly.

## Step 2: Pre-rinse the whole car

Rinse from the roof down to knock off loose dirt and grit. Every particle you remove now is one you won't drag across the paint with your mitt. If you have a foam cannon, let a layer of foam dwell for a few minutes before rinsing; it softens bonded dirt.

## Step 3: Two-bucket hand wash

Load your mitt from the soap bucket, wash one panel in straight lines, then rinse the mitt in the clean-water bucket before going back for more soap. Start at the roof and finish with the lower doors and bumpers, which are the dirtiest. Swap to a second mitt for the lower sections if you can.

## Step 4: Decontaminate with clay

After washing, run your fingertips over the paint inside a thin plastic sandwich bag. If it feels gritty, the surface has bonded contamination that washing won't remove. Spray clay lubricant on a small section and glide the clay across it with light pressure until it moves smoothly. Fold the clay often to expose a clean face, and throw it away if you drop it.

## Step 5: Dry without scratching

Use a large, plush drying towel and pat or drag it gently across each panel; don't scrub. A leaf blower or car dryer is handy for mirrors, grilles and badges, where water likes to hide and drip later.

## Step 6: Polish to remove swirls

Polish is what removes the fine swirls and haze that make paint look dull. By hand, work a small amount into a 2 ft by 2 ft section with a foam [applicator pad](/top-polish-applicator-2023) using overlapping passes, then buff off. A dual-action [polishing machine](/best-polishing-machines-2026) does the same job far faster and more evenly, and it's the single biggest upgrade for home detailers.

## Step 7: Protect the paint

Polish leaves the paint clean but bare. Lock in the finish with a layer of [protection](/paint-protection-2026): a carnauba wax for warmth and gloss, a synthetic sealant for longer life, or a ceramic spray for the best water beading and durability. Apply thin, let it haze as the label directs, and buff off with a fresh towel.

## Step 8: Glass, trim and tires

Clean glass inside and out with a dedicated glass cleaner and a waffle-weave towel. Treat faded plastic trim with a trim restorer and finish tires with a water-based dressing so they look rich, not greasy.

## Step 9: Detail the interior

Work top to bottom. Dust vents and the dashboard with a soft brush, vacuum seats and carpets thoroughly, and pull the [floor mats](/best-car-carpets-2023) out to clean them separately. Wipe hard surfaces with an [interior cleaner](/best-car-interior-cleaner), treat stains on fabric with a [carpet cleaner](/best-car-carpet-cleaner), and use a dedicated [leather cleaner](/best-leather-cleaners-2026) and conditioner on leather seats.

## How often should you do this?

A full nine-step detail two to four times a year is plenty for most cars. In between, a two-bucket wash every couple of weeks and a quick top-up of spray sealant every month or two will keep the finish looking close to how you left it.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Washing in direct sun.** Soap and water dry on hot panels and leave spots that are hard to remove.
- **Using dish soap.** It strips wax and can dry out rubber and plastic trim.
- **One bucket, one towel.** Reusing a dirty mitt or towel is the main cause of swirl marks.
- **Skipping the clay.** Polish and wax won't bond properly to contaminated paint.
