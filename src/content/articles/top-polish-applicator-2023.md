The pad you polish with has as much influence on the result as the polish itself. A pad that's too aggressive leaves haze; one that's too soft won't remove the swirls you're targeting. Get the pairing right and even a basic polish can transform tired paint.

## Pad materials

**Foam** is the most common and the most forgiving. It comes in different densities, usually color-coded by the manufacturer:
- *Cutting pads* are firm and remove heavier swirls and oxidation.
- *Polishing pads* are medium and refine the finish after cutting or handle light defects on their own.
- *Finishing pads* are soft and used to apply glazes, waxes and sealants.

**Microfiber pads** cut faster than foam and are popular for correcting harder clear coats, but they need cleaning more often during use.

**Wool pads** cut aggressively and are mostly used with rotary polishers by experienced detailers.

**Hand applicators** are small foam or microfiber discs for applying polish or wax by hand. They're cheap and perfect for small jobs and touch-ups.

## Flat versus hex-pattern pads

Hex-pattern (waffle) foam pads have a textured face that traps product and helps it spread evenly while staying cooler. Flat pads give a little more cut and are easier to clean. Both work well; it's mostly a matter of preference.

## Matching pads to your polisher

Check the backing plate on your [polishing machine](/best-polishing-machines-2026). Pads should be the same size as the plate or slightly larger, never smaller. Drill-mounted pad kits are an inexpensive way to try machine polishing with a cordless drill, though a proper DA polisher is safer on paint.

## Tips for better results

- Use one pad per panel or two when correcting, and clean pads with a brush or compressed air as you go.
- Start with the least aggressive pad and polish combination that works, and only step up if needed.
- Wash pads in warm water with a pad cleaner after each session and let them dry face-down.
- Keep separate pads for polish and for wax so you don't contaminate your protection layer.
