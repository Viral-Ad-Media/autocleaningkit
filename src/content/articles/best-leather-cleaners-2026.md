Leather seats look and feel great when they're cared for and age badly when they aren't. Body oils, sweat, denim dye and sun exposure slowly dry the leather out, and that's when cracking starts. A good leather cleaner and conditioner, used a few times a year, is all it takes to keep seats soft and looking new.

## Know your leather

Most modern car leather is **coated (finished) leather**. It has a protective topcoat, and that coat is what you're cleaning. Some luxury cars use **semi-aniline or aniline leather**, which is softer and more absorbent and needs gentler products. Many cars also use **synthetic leather** (vinyl or PU), which doesn't need conditioning but still benefits from a gentle cleaner. Check your owner's manual if you're unsure.

## Cleaner, conditioner or 2-in-1?

**Dedicated cleaners** lift dirt from the grain without stripping the finish.

**Conditioners** replenish moisture and add UV protection after cleaning.

**2-in-1 products** clean and condition in one step. They're convenient for regular maintenance, though a separate cleaner and conditioner usually does a better job on very dirty seats.

## What to look for

- A **pH-balanced** formula that's safe on automotive leather
- **No harsh solvents or alcohol**, which dry leather out
- A **non-greasy finish**. Seats shouldn't feel slippery or leave residue on clothes.
- **UV protection** if your car sits in the sun

## How to clean and condition leather seats

1. **Vacuum** the seats, using a soft brush attachment to get into the seams and perforations.
2. **Test** your cleaner on a hidden area first.
3. **Apply the cleaner** to a soft brush or microfiber applicator, not straight onto the seat, especially on perforated leather.
4. **Work gently** in small sections. A soft horsehair brush lifts dirt from the grain without scratching.
5. **Wipe off** the dirty residue with a clean, damp microfiber towel.
6. **Condition** with a thin, even layer, let it absorb, then buff off any excess.

## How often?

Clean and condition every three to four months, and wipe the seats with a damp microfiber cloth in between. Light-colored leather and heavily used driver's seats may need attention more often.
