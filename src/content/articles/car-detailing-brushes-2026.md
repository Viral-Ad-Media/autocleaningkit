Microfiber towels are great on flat panels, but a car is full of places a towel can't reach: emblems, grilles, air vents, seat seams, lug nuts and the gaps around trim. That's what detailing brushes are for. A small set of the right brushes will lift dirt out of these spots without scratching, and they're one of the cheapest upgrades you can make to your kit.

## What to look for in a detailing brush

**Bristle type.** Natural boar's hair is the classic choice for exterior work. It holds soapy water well and is soft enough for emblems, badges and painted surfaces. Synthetic bristles are more durable and easier to clean, and they're ideal for interiors, wheels and tougher grime.

**No exposed metal.** Look for brushes where the ferrule (the band holding the bristles) is wrapped or recessed. A bare metal ferrule can scratch paint or interior plastics the moment you're careless.

**A mix of sizes.** Thin brushes get into vents and seams; wider ones cover grilles and wheel faces quickly. A set with three to five sizes covers almost every job.

**Comfortable handles.** You'll be holding these for a while, so a grippy handle that doesn't get slippery when wet makes a real difference.

## Inside versus outside

Keep separate brushes for the interior and the exterior, and another set for wheels. Wheel brushes pick up brake dust, which is abrasive and metallic, and you don't want it anywhere near your dashboard or paint. Color-coding or labeling the handles keeps things simple.

## How to use detailing brushes

1. **On the exterior,** dip the brush in your wash bucket and gently agitate emblems, badges, grilles and window seals, then rinse with water.
2. **On wheels,** spray the wheel cleaner first, then work the brush around lug nuts and into the spokes.
3. **Inside the car,** use a dry brush to loosen dust from vents and switches while you hold a vacuum nozzle next to it. For sticky spots, mist interior cleaner onto the brush, not the surface.

## Caring for your brushes

Rinse brushes thoroughly after every use and let them dry bristles-down so water doesn't sit in the base and loosen the glue. Wash boar's hair brushes with a little car shampoo now and then; they'll last for years with basic care.
