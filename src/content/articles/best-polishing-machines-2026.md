If you've ever polished a car by hand, you know the problem: by the third panel your arm is tired and the results are uneven. A polishing machine fixes both. It spins or oscillates the pad thousands of times a minute, so it removes swirl marks and light scratches in a fraction of the time and with far more consistent results.

## Types of polishing machines

**Dual-action (DA) or random-orbital polishers** spin the pad and move it in an off-center orbit at the same time. That motion spreads heat and makes it very hard to burn through paint, which is why DA polishers are the best choice for beginners and most home detailers.

**Rotary polishers** spin the pad on a single fixed axis. They cut faster and can remove deeper defects, but they build heat quickly and can leave holograms or damage paint in inexperienced hands. They're a pro tool.

**Mini and air polishers** use small 2 to 3 inch pads for tight areas such as A-pillars, mirrors and bumper curves. Air-powered versions need a compressor.

**Budget orbital waxers** are simple, inexpensive machines with large bonnets. They're good for spreading and buffing wax but don't have the power to correct swirls.

## What to look for

- **Variable speed.** Low speeds for spreading product, higher speeds for correcting. A dial with five or six settings is ideal.
- **Throw (orbit size) on DA polishers.** An 8 mm throw is a good all-rounder; larger throws (15 mm and up) cover big panels faster.
- **Weight and balance.** You'll hold the machine for an hour or more, and a well-balanced 5 to 6 lb tool is much easier to control.
- **Backing plate size.** Make sure the pads you want to use fit the backing plate. Five-inch pads on a five-inch plate is the common standard.
- **What's in the box.** Kits that include pads, a spare backing plate and a carry case are better value if you're starting from scratch.

## How to polish with a DA machine

1. Wash and clay the car first. Polishing over grit will add scratches, not remove them.
2. Apply four pea-sized drops of polish to the pad and dab it across a 2 ft by 2 ft section with the machine off.
3. Turn it on at a low speed to spread the product, then raise the speed and work in slow, overlapping passes, first side to side and then up and down.
4. Keep the pad flat and use light, even pressure. Let the machine do the work.
5. Wipe off the residue with a clean microfiber and check the result under good light before moving on.
6. Finish with a wax, sealant or [ceramic protection](/paint-protection-2026) to lock in the gloss.
